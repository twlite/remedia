## Crossfade playback

Crossfade is disabled by default. Opted-in sessions can pass through eligible Opus
packets outside an overlap, and decode stereo 48 kHz PCM when mixing is required. A whole packet must fit before the overlap boundary. Otherwise the decoder warms
from bounded compressed history and trims to the exact source boundary. The sources
mix with a sample-accurate [curve](#crossfade-curves), `equalPower` by default. One output and Opus encoder
remain open across promotions.

Prepared sources keep their existing producer and
bounded PCM buffer after promotion. They continue transcoding rather than reopen
the source or bypass decoded samples. Direct sources can restore passthrough after
temporary volume processing only at matching source and encoder packet boundaries. A gain update that leaves passthrough waits for PCM activation. Restoration can
complete later when the direct source reaches a safe boundary. Seeking disables passthrough restoration for that direct source. Ordinary sessions retain their existing Opus
passthrough selection and allocate no staging decoder or PCM buffer.

### Crossfade curves

`CrossfadeConfig::curve` selects how the two sources are weighted during the overlap.
`CrossfadeConfig::new(duration_ms, prepare_before_ms)` keeps `CrossfadeCurve::EqualPower`;
`.with_curve(CrossfadeCurve::Adaptive)` opts in to the adaptive curve.

```rust
use remedia_core::{CrossfadeConfig, CrossfadeCurve, PlaybackSession};

let session = PlaybackSession::new();
let config = CrossfadeConfig::new(5_000, 30_000)
    .expect("durations are in range")
    .with_curve(CrossfadeCurve::Adaptive);

session.configure_crossfade(Some(config))?;
```

| Curve        | Behavior                                                                                                                         |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `equalPower` | Fixed gains `cos`/`sin` over the window. No analysis. The default.                                                               |
| `adaptive`   | The same gains, plus one bounded, smoothed gain shared by both sources and both channels that follows their loudness difference. |

Both curves keep the first mixed sample equal to the outgoing source and the last equal to the
incoming source, and neither changes the length of the transition.

#### How `adaptive` works

The mixer stays inside the existing PCM worker. It makes no additional decoder, buffer, or allocation,
and does nothing outside the overlap.

1. **Base mix.** Each sample pair is mixed with the equal-power gains.
2. **Measure.** Squared samples of the outgoing source, the incoming source, and the equal-power mix
   are summed per 10 ms hop (480 frames, aligned to the start of the overlap, not to PCM blocks).
   Left and right are summed together, so one level drives one gain. Each hop's mean-square power is
   smoothed with a 150 ms attack and a 300 ms release. A single hop can raise the estimate by at most
   4x, so an isolated transient barely moves it. The mix is measured directly, so correlation between
   the tracks is accounted for.
3. **Target.** The desired output level glides from the outgoing level to the incoming level in decibels
   along a smoothstep. The common gain is the desired level minus the measured mix level, clamped to
   **-6 dB..0 dB**. A positive result would be a boost, so it is never applied. Confidence fades
   the adjustment to zero as either source approaches silence (-70 dBFS is silent, -55 dBFS is
   fully trusted), so noise floors and gaps are never chased.
4. **Smooth.** The gain moves by one-pole smoothing (200 ms) limited to 0.1 dB per hop (10 dB/s), and
   is interpolated linearly in amplitude between hop boundaries. A hop's gain depends only on audio
   already mixed, so there is no feedback loop.
5. **Edges.** The adjustment is weighted to exactly zero for the first and last two hops and
   rises smoothly over 20% of the window, so the output joins the unprocessed neighbouring source
   with no gain step.

The result is independent of how the caller splits the PCM into blocks, down to the bit.

#### Peak handling

Both curves pass the mix through a stereo-linked, zero-latency peak limiter instead of clipping it.
Samples that fit are returned unchanged. A sample that would exceed full scale is attenuated
immediately, and the reduction releases exponentially over about 20 ms. The limiter never reduces by
more than 20 dB, fades its influence out over the last 480 frames of the window, and a final clamp to
`-1.0..=1.0` remains only as a safety net. Previously, over-range samples were hard-clipped. Anything
that fits in range is bit-identical to before.

On two identical near-full-scale sine waves, which sum to 1.39 times full scale at the midpoint, the limiter
produces about 0.6% distortion where clipping produces about 12%.

`CrossfadeConfig` gained a public `curve` field. Code that builds the struct with a literal must set it;
`CrossfadeConfig::new` keeps its signature and `equalPower` behavior.

#### Limits to be aware of

- Levels are RMS of the overlap, without K-weighting or integrated loudness. They approximate, but are not,
  perceived loudness.
- The cut is capped at 6 dB. Tracks more than about 12 dB apart are brought closer, not matched.
- `adaptive` never boosts, so a quiet track is not made louder than it is.
- The limiter has no look-ahead, because the overlap must keep the sample clock of the sources around it.
  A peak is attenuated from the sample where it occurs, which can be audible as a brief duck on
  isolated full-scale clicks and as slight compression when two dense, loud masters are summed.
- The estimates need roughly the first 150 ms of the overlap, and fades near the 1 s minimum
  give the adjustment little time to act.
- Volume and filter graphs apply after the mix, as before.

Measured costs and reproducible comparisons are in [Performance](/docs/core/performance#crossfade-curves).

### Preparation timing

Defaults are a 5000 ms fade and a 30000 ms preparation lead. The engine emits
`prepareRequested` approximately 35000 ms before a known-duration source ends.
Unknown-duration inputs do not receive end-based requests. Resolve upcoming sources
when requested rather than caching expiring URLs minutes ahead. Fades support
1000–30000 ms. Preparation leads support 5000–120000 ms.

### Stage identity and buffering

`stageNext(source, { expectedGeneration, entryId, queueRevision, sourceRevision })`
identifies a queued occurrence, including repeated tracks. Repeating the same
identity is idempotent. Higher queue revisions replace previous decisions. Higher
source revisions refresh the same entry. Older revisions cannot overwrite a newer
stage, including after cancellation. Staging opens an independent decoder with a
bounded two-second PCM buffer and backpressure. `stageReady`, `stageFailed` and
`stageInvalidated` report its lifecycle.

### Late stages and interrupted transitions

At the fade boundary, an unready, missing, expired or failed stage is ignored and
ordinary source completion continues. The mixer never waits for an incoming frame.
An underflow during overlap cancels the transition and restores the outgoing
source. `transitionCancelled` identifies the interrupted incoming generation. Pause freezes overlap progress and preserves preparation. Resume checks readiness
and expiry.

Seek clears preparation, advances the source generation to fence delayed requests,
and recalculates the request threshold. This generation change applies only to
crossfade sessions. The source is retained and no new start event is emitted. Stop
cancels decoders and blocked buffer operations. Volume and filters apply to the
mixed PCM. Transport recovery retains the same playback engine.

### Promotion events

The incoming source emits `playbackStarted` when overlap begins. After the fade,
the outgoing source emits `playbackCompleted` with
`continuation: { type: 'promoted', generation, entryId }`. That continuation means
the next source is already playing: consumers must not call `play()` for it.
The final source completes normally without continuation. Generations identify
sources independently of transport recovery and the continuous output clock.
